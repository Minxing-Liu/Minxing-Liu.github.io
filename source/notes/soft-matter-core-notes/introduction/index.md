<h2 id="Core-Picture">Core Picture</h2><p>Soft matter is not one material. It is a broad class including colloids, polymers, liquid crystals, surfactants, gels, foams, emulsions, biological membranes, and active matter. The common features are mesoscopic length scales, weak interactions, strong thermal fluctuations, and large response to fields and boundaries.</p>
<h2 id="Why-Soft">Why Soft</h2><p>Soft matter has much smaller stiffness than ordinary crystals or metals. Small energies can produce large deformation, so entropy and thermal fluctuations are central.</p>
<div class="equation-block">
  <div><span class="eq-left">thermal</span><span class="eq-op">=</span><span>\(k_B T\approx4.1\,\mathrm{pN\,nm}\) at room temperature</span></div>
  <div><span class="eq-left">softness</span><span class="eq-op">=</span><span>interaction energy often comparable to \(k_B T\)</span></div>
  <div><span class="eq-left">free energy</span><span class="eq-op">=</span><span>\(F=U-TS\)</span></div>
</div>

<p>If the interaction is only a few k_B T, structures can fluctuate, rearrange, and self-assemble. If it is much larger, structures can become trapped and irreversible.</p>
<h2 id="Mesoscopic-Scale">Mesoscopic Scale</h2><p>Soft-matter structures often lie between molecular and macroscopic scales. They are large enough to image but small enough for Brownian motion to matter.</p>
<div class="equation-block">
  <div><span class="eq-left">colloid</span><span class="eq-op">=</span><span>\(10\,\mathrm{nm}\sim10\,\mu\mathrm{m}\)</span></div>
  <div><span class="eq-left">polymer</span><span class="eq-op">=</span><span>chain conformation controlled by entropy</span></div>
  <div><span class="eq-left">membrane</span><span class="eq-op">=</span><span>2D fluid surface with bending elasticity</span></div>
</div>

<h2 id="Dimensionless-Numbers">Dimensionless Numbers</h2><p>Soft matter problems are often solved by comparing physical effects.</p>
<div class="equation-block">
  <div><span class="eq-left">Re</span><span class="eq-op">=</span><span>\(\rho U L/\eta\)</span></div>
  <div><span class="eq-left">Pe</span><span class="eq-op">=</span><span>\(UL/D\)</span></div>
  <div><span class="eq-left">Ca</span><span class="eq-op">=</span><span>\(\eta U/\gamma\)</span></div>
  <div><span class="eq-left">energy</span><span class="eq-op">=</span><span>\(U_{\mathrm{interaction}}/k_B T\)</span></div>
</div>

<p>Re compares inertia with viscosity. Pe compares advection with diffusion. Ca compares viscous stress with surface tension. U/k_B T tells whether thermal fluctuations can reorganize the structure.</p>
<h2 id="Course-Thread">Course Thread</h2><ol>
<li>Fluid dynamics: low-Reynolds-number viscous motion.</li>
<li>Elasticity: reversible deformation and elastic free energy.</li>
<li>Brownian motion: thermal noise, damping, and diffusion.</li>
<li>Phases and self-assembly: entropy and interaction-driven structures.</li>
<li>Interfaces and membranes: surface tension, curvature, and bending energy.</li>
</ol>
<h2 id="Exam-Cue">Exam Cue</h2><p>Start with four questions: Is the energy close to k_B T? Is the length scale mesoscopic? Is the dynamics viscous or inertial? Is the structure controlled mainly by energy minimization or entropy maximization?</p>
